Grow your own medicinal herb garden

A 34-page PDF guide to growing ten medicinal herbs from seed, from sowing to a jar of dried herbs.

Why this guide exists

A seed packet or a seed kit gives you seeds. What decides whether you get a harvest is everything else: which seeds need light, which need a month in the fridge, which die if you move them, when to pick and how to dry. That is what this guide is.

The ten herbs

  • Calendula (Calendula officinalis)
  • Chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla)
  • California poppy (Eschscholzia californica)
  • Chicory (Cichorium intybus)
  • Yarrow (Achillea millefolium)
  • Feverfew (Tanacetum parthenium)
  • Echinacea (Echinacea purpurea)
  • Evening primrose (Oenothera biennis)
  • Marshmallow (Althaea officinalis)
  • Lavender (Lavandula angustifolia)

What’s inside

  • Where to buy the seeds: single packets with prices, or one kit with all ten
  • A setup on a budget: containers, a homemade seed-starting mix, light and warmth
  • Three germination tricks: seeds that need light, seeds that need a fake winter, the seed that hates being moved
  • From seedling to garden: watering, thinning, potting up, a 7-day hardening-off schedule
  • A sowing calendar timed from your last frost date
  • Two pages per herb: sun, soil, sowing, watering, growing in pots, feeding, problems and fixes, harvest, storing, saving seed
  • Harvest, drying and storage: when to pick each herb, three drying methods, how to tell it is dry
  • Bonus — the herb feeding plan: which herbs should never be fed, three homemade liquid feeds, rooting lavender cuttings in willow water
  • Printable planner: sowing log and garden map

This is a growing guide. It makes no health claims and is not medical advice.
