How to grow echinacea from seed

The pink coneflower bees and goldfinches love — it needs a fake winter before it sprouts well.

MediumPerennialEchinacea purpurea · purple coneflower · Daisy family (Asteraceae)

At a glance

Life cycle
Perennial, zones 3–9
Difficulty
Medium
Sun
Full sun to light shade
Soil
Average, well-drained; drought tolerant once established
Sowing depth
⅛–¼ in (3–6 mm)
Germination
10–20 days at 65–70°F (18–21°C), better after a cold treatment
When to sow
Cold-treat the seed, then sow indoors 8–10 weeks before last frost — or sow outdoors in late autumn and let winter do it
Spacing
18–24 in (45–60 cm)
Height
2–4 ft (60–120 cm)
Flowers
Usually the second summer

Step by step

  1. Cold treatment: mix the seed with a little damp sand or put it in a damp paper towel, seal in a bag, and keep it in the fridge for about 4 weeks.
  2. Sow ⅛–¼ inch deep in pots of seed mix at room temperature. Seedlings appear in two to three weeks.
  3. Plant out after the last frost, 18–24 inches apart, in full sun.
  4. Do not expect many flowers the first year — it is building roots. From the second summer it blooms for weeks.

Harvest and drying

Flowers and leaves are cut in summer. Roots are traditionally taken only from plants at least three years old, dug in autumn.

Saving seed

Leave the spiky seed heads standing over winter — goldfinches eat from them. Collect some seed once the cones are dark and dry.

A bit of history

Echinacea species were among the most widely used plants of the Great Plains peoples. From the late 1800s it entered American commercial herbal products and is still one of the best-known herbs in the US.
