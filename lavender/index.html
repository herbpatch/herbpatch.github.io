How to grow lavender from seed

The slowest one from seed — but one plant can live and flower for ten years or more.

PatientPerennial shrubLavandula angustifolia · English lavender · Mint family (Lamiaceae)

At a glance

Life cycle
Perennial shrub, zones 5–9
Difficulty
Patient
Sun
Full sun — 6 hours or more
Soil
Lean, very well-drained, neutral to alkaline; wet roots kill it
Sowing depth
Surface-sow and press in — light helps germination
Germination
14–28 days at 65–70°F (18–21°C), sometimes longer and uneven; a cold treatment helps
When to sow
Cold-treat 3–4 weeks, then sow indoors 10–12 weeks before last frost
Spacing
18–24 in (45–60 cm)
Height
1–3 ft (30–90 cm)
Flowers
A few spikes the first year; full bloom from year two

Step by step

  1. Cold treatment: seed in a damp paper towel, sealed bag, fridge, 3–4 weeks.
  2. Sow on the surface of a gritty seed mix, press in, keep at room temperature in bright light. Expect sprouts over two to four weeks — sometimes more.
  3. Water seedlings from below and let the top dry between waterings. Soggy mix is the number one killer.
  4. Plant out after frost in the sunniest, best-drained spot you have — a raised bed or a slope is ideal. Once established, water rarely.

Watering

Seedlings: water from below. Established plants: water deeply but rarely, and let the soil dry out completely between waterings. Overwatering kills more lavender than cold does.

Growing in pots

Excellent in a clay pot 12–16 in (30–40 cm) wide with gritty mix (add a third of coarse sand or grit). In cold regions, move the pot against a sheltered wall in winter.

Feeding

None. In acidic soil, a light sprinkle of garden lime once a year helps.

Problems and fixes

  • Root rot — heavy or wet soil. Plant high, on a mound or in a raised bed.
  • Woody, sprawling plants — trim by a third every year after flowering, never into bare wood.
  • Winter losses — wet cold is the killer, not dry cold. Good drainage matters most.

Harvest and drying

Cut stems in the morning when about half the buds on a spike are open. Hang bundles upside down in a dark, airy place. After flowering, trim the plant by about a third — but never cut into bare old wood.

Storing

Dried buds in a sealed jar. The scent lasts about a year.

Saving seed

Seeds sit inside the dried flower spikes. Rub the spikes over a paper plate to collect them.

A bit of history

Romans scented their baths and laundry with lavender, and its name is often traced to the Latin lavare, “to wash.” It has been a linen-cupboard herb in Europe ever since.
