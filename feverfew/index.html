How to grow feverfew from seed

Clouds of tiny white daisies with a sharp, bitter scent that many garden pests dislike.

EasyShort-lived perennialTanacetum parthenium · featherfew · Daisy family (Asteraceae)

At a glance

Life cycle
Short-lived perennial, zones 5–9; self-seeds freely
Difficulty
Easy
Sun
Full sun to part shade
Soil
Average, well-drained
Sowing depth
Surface-sow and press in — the seeds need light to germinate
Germination
7–14 days at 65–70°F (18–21°C)
When to sow
Indoors 6–8 weeks before last frost, or direct sow in spring
Spacing
12–15 in (30–38 cm)
Height
1–2 ft (30–60 cm)
Flowers
First summer if started early

Step by step

  1. Sow on the surface of moist seed mix and press in lightly. Keep warm and bright.
  2. Pot on seedlings when they have two sets of true leaves.
  3. Plant out after the last frost, 12–15 inches apart. It tolerates partial shade better than most herbs here.
  4. Cut the plant back by half after the first flush of flowers for a second round.

Watering

Moderate. Let the top inch dry between waterings; it tolerates short droughts.

Growing in pots

Grows well in an 8–10 in (20–25 cm) pot on a patio or balcony.

Feeding

None, or a little compost in spring.

Problems and fixes

  • Aphids — spray off with water.
  • Plants fade after 2–3 years — keep a few self-sown seedlings as replacements.
  • Seedlings everywhere — deadhead if you do not want them.

Harvest and drying

Leaves can be picked once the plant is established; flowers when they open. Hang stems to dry in small bundles.

Storing

Dried leaves and flowers in a sealed jar; use within a year.

Saving seed

It drops plenty of seed on its own — you will find seedlings nearby every spring. Pot them up or weed them out.

A bit of history

The name comes from the Latin febrifugia. Feverfew has been part of European cottage gardens and folk practice for centuries.
