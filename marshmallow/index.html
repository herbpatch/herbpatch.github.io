How to grow marshmallow from seed

Yes, the original marshmallow sweet was made from this plant’s root.

MediumPerennialAlthaea officinalis · marsh mallow · Mallow family (Malvaceae)

At a glance

Life cycle
Perennial, zones 3–9
Difficulty
Medium
Sun
Full sun to part shade
Soil
Moist, rich — it grows wild in damp meadows and marsh edges
Sowing depth
¼ in (6 mm)
Germination
2–4 weeks at 60–70°F (16–21°C), often uneven; a cold treatment helps
When to sow
Cold-treat the seed for 2–4 weeks, then sow indoors 8 weeks before last frost — or sow outdoors in autumn
Spacing
18–24 in (45–60 cm)
Height
3–5 ft (90–150 cm)
Flowers
Usually the second summer

Step by step

  1. Put the seed in a damp paper towel in a sealed bag in the fridge for 2–4 weeks. Its hard coat needs the cold.
  2. Sow ¼ inch deep in pots of moist seed mix. Be patient — seedlings can trickle up over several weeks.
  3. Plant out after frost, 18–24 inches apart, in the dampest sunny corner you have. Water in dry spells; it hates drying out.
  4. It grows tall. Give it the back of the bed.

Watering

High — keep the soil evenly moist all season. A layer of mulch helps.

Growing in pots

Only in a big container (5 gallons / 20 litres or more) with daily watering in summer.

Feeding

It likes richer soil than the others: compost in spring is enough.

Problems and fixes

  • Rust — orange spots under the leaves. Remove affected leaves, give more air.
  • Japanese beetles and flea beetles — hand-pick; plants usually outgrow the damage.
  • Tall stems falling over in wind — stake them.

Harvest and drying

Leaves and pale pink flowers are picked in summer. Roots are traditionally dug in autumn of the second year, washed, sliced and dried.

Storing

Dried root pieces and leaves in a sealed jar away from moisture.

Saving seed

The round, flat seed heads look like tiny cheese wheels. Pick them when they turn brown.

A bit of history

The plant’s root is full of a slippery gel. French confectioners of the 1800s whipped it with sugar and egg white into pâte de guimauve — the original marshmallow. Modern marshmallows use gelatin instead.
