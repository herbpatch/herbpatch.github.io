How to grow chicory from seed

Sky-blue flowers that open in the morning and close by afternoon — and a root people once roasted like coffee.

EasyPerennialCichorium intybus · blue sailors · Daisy family (Asteraceae)

At a glance

Life cycle
Perennial, zones 3–9
Difficulty
Easy
Sun
Full sun
Soil
Average to poor, deep and well-drained — it grows a long taproot
Sowing depth
¼ in (6 mm)
Germination
5–14 days at 60–70°F (16–21°C)
When to sow
Direct sow in early spring, or in late summer
Spacing
8–12 in (20–30 cm)
Height
2–4 ft (60–120 cm) when flowering
Flowers
Usually in the second year

Step by step

  1. Loosen the soil deeply — the taproot goes down a long way. Sow seeds ¼ inch deep in rows or small groups.
  2. Keep moist until seedlings appear, then thin to 8–12 inches apart.
  3. Chicory needs almost no care. Water in long dry spells only.
  4. In the first year it makes a rosette of leaves; the tall flowering stems usually come in the second summer.

Watering

Moderate. Water deeply now and then rather than a little every day, so the taproot goes down. Drought-tolerant once established.

Growing in pots

Possible only in a deep pot, 12 in (30 cm) or more, because of the taproot. Better in the ground.

Feeding

None, or a little compost at planting.

Problems and fixes

  • Slugs on young leaves.
  • Aphids — spray off with water.
  • Self-seeding into lawns and paths — cut stems after flowering.

Harvest and drying

Young leaves in spring are edible but bitter. Roots are traditionally dug in autumn of the first year, washed, sliced and dried.

Storing

Dried root pieces in a sealed jar. Roast before storing if you want the coffee-style flavour.

Saving seed

Seed forms in the flower heads along the stems in late summer. It self-seeds and can spread — cut spent stems if you want to keep it in place.

A bit of history

Egyptians and Romans ate chicory as a vegetable. In 19th-century France, and later in New Orleans, the roasted root was mixed into coffee to stretch it — a tradition New Orleans still keeps.
