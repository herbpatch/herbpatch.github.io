How to grow chamomile from seed

Seeds as fine as dust, flowers in two months, and one of the oldest tea herbs in the world.

EasyAnnualMatricaria chamomilla · German chamomile · Daisy family (Asteraceae)

At a glance

Life cycle
Annual, self-seeding
Difficulty
Easy
Sun
Full sun; light shade in hot climates
Soil
Average to poor, well-drained
Sowing depth
Surface-sow and press in — the tiny seeds need light to germinate
Germination
7–14 days at 60–68°F (16–20°C)
When to sow
Direct sow in early spring or autumn, or indoors 6 weeks before last frost
Spacing
6–8 in (15–20 cm)
Height
12–24 in (30–60 cm)
Flowers
About 6–10 weeks after sowing

Step by step

  1. Mix the dust-fine seed with a spoon of dry sand so you can see where it lands. Scatter it on raked soil and press it down with a board — do not cover it.
  2. Mist or water gently so the seed does not wash away. Keep the surface damp until sprouts appear.
  3. Thin to 6–8 inches apart. Chamomile does fine in poor soil; rich soil gives more leaves and fewer flowers.
  4. Most seed packets labelled “chamomile” are German chamomile, an annual. Roman chamomile is a low perennial ground cover — check your packet.

Watering

Keep the seed bed damp until plants are a few inches tall. After that, water when the top inch of soil is dry. Established plants handle short dry spells.

Growing in pots

Good in pots and window boxes: several plants in an 8 in (20 cm) pot. Sow straight into the pot.

Feeding

None. Poor soil gives more flowers.

Problems and fixes

  • Plants flop over — sow them closer so they hold each other up.
  • It finishes flowering in summer heat — sow a new small batch every 3–4 weeks in spring for a longer harvest.
  • Aphids — spray off with water.

Harvest and drying

Pick the flower heads on a dry morning when the white petals lie flat or just start to bend back. Keep picking every few days. Dry them on a screen in the shade for one to two weeks, then store in a glass jar.

Storing

Whole dried flower heads in a sealed jar, dark cupboard. Keeps its scent about a year.

Saving seed

Leave some heads on the plant until they turn brown and crumbly; it will also sow itself for next year.

A bit of history

Ancient Egyptians associated chamomile with the sun, and it has been one of Europe’s everyday tea herbs for centuries. In Beatrix Potter’s 1902 tale, Peter Rabbit’s mother puts him to bed with a dose of chamomile tea.
